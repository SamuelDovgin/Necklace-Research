# Marquise tennis necklace buying brief

The preferred design became a continuous front of east-west single marquise lab diamonds with a plain adjustable cable-chain back. The inspiration’s pointed leaf-like links, close spacing, and collarbone fit were the main visual preferences. The photo-based 6–7 × 3–3.5 mm estimate and 15–16 in fit are starting points for CAD and a real fit check. [Reference photos and full guide](records/marquise.html)

## Quote these two constructions

| Option | Starting specification | Main checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adjustable partial tennis | 7–9 in of diamond front, start at 8 in. Approx. 6 × 3 mm stones, with 5 × 2.5 and 7 × 3.5 mm alternates. Solid 14K white and yellow gold quoted separately. Plain cable back adjustable 15–18 in. | Show the actual clasp positions and panel centering at both lengths. Low-profile articulated links, protected points, secure lobster clasp, and smooth symmetrical joins. |
| Full east-west tennis | Fixed 16 in starting point, closely linked stones, quote 6 × 3 mm and 7 × 3.5 mm. Solid 14K white and yellow gold. | Quote stone count, total ctw, finished metal weight, CAD cost and movement video. Confirm how the necklace drapes at the wearer’s preferred length. |

The original idea of a 40–55% reduction in diamond usage was a coverage intuition. Eight inches of a 16-inch necklace is half its nominal length, while cost depends on the actual stone count, labor, hardware, and metal. Confirm minimum wearable length and diamond panel length in the written quote.

## What to compare across replies

Ask for front, back and side CAD, actual marquise count and sizes, total ctw, stone quality documentation, finished alloy-only grams, chain gauge and length, adjustments, prong protection, articulation, warranty or remake terms, and a movement video. Specify individual stones and continuous tips if quoting that design. The dossier also preserves the separate cluster-style search, buyer feedback on StarsGem, and old promotions from Provence, Julysnow, StarsGem and Fiorese.
